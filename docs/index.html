How The Oracle Works

Each morning your almanac shows a single reading, drawn from the true position of the sky and the meaning tradition has long assigned to it. No guessing. Precise astronomy, a codified craft.

The promise

Calculated, not conjured

A reading is cast in three stages. You tell us where and when you were born. We calculate the sky, yours at birth and today's above it, to the precision a professional astrologer uses. We then read those positions into words through the tradition's tables of correspondence. No artificial intelligence writes the reading. The same chart on the same day always gives the same words, because it is derived, not invented.

Stage I · Onboarding

What you tell us when you order

A birth chart is the sky from one place, at one instant. To cast it we need that instant and that place, the same details any astrologer asks for.

Why time and place matter. Two people born the same day can rise in different signs, with planets in different houses, depending on the hour and city. Without them, a reading falls back to a generic horoscope. This device is made not to be that.

Stage II · The chart

How we place the Moon and planets, precisely

Positions come from an ephemeris, the same source (Swiss Ephemeris, from NASA/JPL) professional astrologers rely on. A table of daily positions spanning centuries lives on the device, so every reading is accurate to a fraction of a degree, offline.

1

Birth time to Universal Time

Your city gives the time zone and any daylight saving in force that day. We convert to UT, then to a Julian Day, the continuous day-count astronomy uses.

2

Ephemeris to ecliptic longitude

For that instant we read the longitude of the Sun, Moon and Mercury through Pluto, with the lunar node. A longitude of 0 to 360° places each body in a sign, thirty degrees each.

3

Ascendant and houses

From local sidereal time, the tilt of the ecliptic and your latitude we find the Ascendant, the degree rising in the east, and the twelve house cusps that divide your chart into life areas.

4

Moon phase and retrogrades

The Sun to Moon elongation gives the phase and exact illuminated fraction. A planet's motion across successive days shows when it turns retrograde.

5

The day's transits to your chart

Each morning we set today's planets against your natal ones. When the angle between them meets a known aspect (0°, 60°, 90°, 120°, 180°) within a small tolerance, the orb, that transit is active.

6

The day's ruling transit

Active transits are weighed by the tightness of the orb and the standing of the planets. The strongest becomes the heart of today's reading.

What "an aspect within orb" means

# Longitudes in degrees. A "square" is a 90° separation.
sep        = angular_distance(transit_planet.lon, natal_planet.lon)   # 0–180°
target     = 90.0                                                     # square
orb        = abs(sep - target)
is_active  = orb <= 6.0                                               # within tolerance
strength   = (6.0 - orb) * weight(transit_planet) * weight(natal_planet)

The numbers above are illustrative; production orbs follow the tradition (tighter for minor bodies, wider for luminaries) and are reviewed by a practitioner.

Planet, house and aspect chose the subject and tone. The tables gave the keywords. A fixed sentence gave the grammar. A seed chose among equal phrasings, so no two recent days repeat. The stone line comes straight from its correspondence row.

The same chart gives the same reading. Derived from your chart and the day by fixed rules, it can be checked. Hand the same birth data and date to any astrologer with the same tables, and they reach the same reading. That is what sets this apart from a fortune cookie.

A word on honesty

Reflection, not prediction

The Oracle never claims to know the future, and never offers medical, financial or legal advice. A filter holds every reading to this. The astronomy is literally true. The meanings belong to a long human tradition of reading the sky as a mirror. We offer it in that spirit: a considered prompt for the day, closer to a stoic maxim than a horoscope that pretends to foretell events.

Provenance

Where the craft comes from

The astronomy rests on published tables. The meanings rest on named, respected sources. A practicing astrologer reviews the correspondence tables before they ship.

  • Swiss Ephemeris, Astrodienst, from NASA/JPL. Planetary positions.
  • Jean Meeus, Astronomical Algorithms. Phase, sidereal time, obliquity.
  • Robert Hand, Planets in Transit. Transit interpretation.
  • Dane Rudhyar, The Lunation Cycle. Moon-phase meaning.
  • Ptolemy, Tetrabiblos. Classical aspects and dignities.
  • Traditional crystal correspondences. Stone intentions.
